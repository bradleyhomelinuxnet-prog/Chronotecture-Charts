(Origin and Significane of the Great Pyramid: 116) In Lost Scriptures this authors cites numerous traditions and historic records that purport that the monument's hidden function was that of a calendar from the distant past to the future. But unfortunately for Taylor, theories always precede application and ideas are conceptualized within the minds of men who often die before others can demonstrate their mathematical fact. Taylor also published another work entitled The Battle of the Standards, serving to show that the Great Pyramid recorded within its dimensions the historic, origin and eternal standards mankind was entrusted to use in the beginning and will resume in the future, measures in time, space and volume.

While the intellectual elitists ridiculed Taylor, his work served to awaken a giant in the world of science. This was the Astronomer Royal for Scotland named Charles Piazzi Smyth, a Christian who believed that Taylor's theories merited further research. He was a reputable scholar who had held the position of Astronomer Royal since 1845. Smyth wrote John Taylor and the two began a fasinating dialogue and became close friends. Taylor was very old and he heartily believed that God had visited mercy on him in the form of Piazzi Smyth, who would take his life work and carry the torch of revelation on the Great Pyramid beyond his death. A tremendous relief to a man who had spent his life attempting to tell the world what he knew.

The fateful year of 1864 AD came and the aged John Taylor died. His private notes and much of his research notes were willed by the family to Piazzi Smyth, who with his wife, then travelled to Egypt in this same year in a privately funded mission to the Great Pyramid in the hope of proving John Taylor correct. (The Great Pyramid: Smyth 651, The Great Pyramid: Davidson, 149) Smyth took with him instrument by which he could make meticulous measurements throughout the structure (Signs in the Sky 52) which resulted in the discovery of far more evidence of the divine purpose of the monument than even Taylor had imagined. Seeing the dimensions for himself, Smyth was immediately persuaded in the biblical importance of this arcane relic standing in Egypt and amazingly, his measurements revealed an architectural awareness of the size, dimensions and density of the Earth, its distance from the sun, equitorial dimater and many other amazing facts. (Feats and Wisdom of the Ancients 48)

AS Smyth was busy in 1864 crawling through the interior of the structure measuring almost every conceivable angle and distance in passages and chambers, another Scotsman made a stunning revelation. His name was Robert Menzies, who had traced his own name to the earlier Hebrew Manesseh, and he had been inspired by Taylor's book and other writings on the Great Pyramid. Menzies wrote a letter to Piazzi Smyth and this letter was received in 1865 by Smyth and his wife at the Great Pyramid site. Like John Taylor, Robert Menzies had never visited Egypt, his information and theories coing from the research of others and by inspiration. Menzies explained to Smyth that he believed the Great Pyramid's Ascendant Passage with its GRand Gallery was actually a linear calendar of the Christian history of the world from the Crucifixion to the Last Days, each inch representing a year. Smyth was impressed with the idea, however, they concept was not taken seriously until something most unusual occurred.

In Menzies' letter was provided detail-specific architectural facts about the interior dimensions of the structure that were only at that exact same time discovered by Piazzi Smyth. Knowing that Menzies had never been to the pyramid site, Smyth double-checked the measrements he had made and made a search through all the books published and found no references at all existing in the writing research of the monument by which Menzies could have obtained this data. (The Great Pyramid: Smyth 461-462)
